# Brand Voice Guide Process

> Derive the brand's voice from its existing materials and the owner's preferences, write it as a few concrete rules with examples, and get human approval before using it.

## Why derive instead of invent

A voice invented by an agent may sound polished and still be wrong for the brand, and different agents will invent different ones. Customers notice when an ad, a product page and a chat reply sound like three companies. A short guide based on evidence keeps every channel recognisably the same and gives reviewers something to check against.

## Steps

1. **Collect samples.** Gather existing text the brand has published or sent: store pages, social captions, emails, chat replies written by staff. Note the source of each.
2. **Ask the owner.** How should the brand sound? Which words do they like or dislike? Who do they not want to sound like? Record answers verbatim.
3. **Look for patterns.** Formal or casual, long or short sentences, technical or plain, humour or none, how customers are addressed, which language or languages and regional spellings are used.
4. **Draft rules.** Write three to six rules, each with a "do" example and a "don't" example taken from or modelled on the samples.
5. **Add channel notes.** How the same voice adapts for an ad headline, a product page, a short video and a chat message.
6. **Get approval.** Mark the guide as draft until a responsible human signs off.

## Rules that apply regardless of voice

- Voice never overrides accuracy. A lively tone does not permit exaggeration; see [[Verified Claims Policy]].
- No pressure tactics, no invented scarcity, no mocking of competitors.
- Respectful and patient in chat, as required by [[Objection Handling Approach]].

## Where it is used

Attach the guide to every [[Creative Brief Template]] and reference it in the [[Product Page Checklist]].

## Facts to confirm from the store

- Existing published copy to use as samples.
- Languages and markets served.
- From the owner: preferred tone, banned words and approval of the draft.

## Check before you hand off

- Samples and their sources are listed.
- Each rule has a do and a don't example.
- The guide's status, draft or approved, is stated at the top.
- No rule encourages unverified or exaggerated claims.
