# Offer Definition Worksheet

> Write each offer as a short structured statement of audience, problem, product, proof, terms and next step, where every factual line is copied from a verified source.

## Why structure the offer

An offer is more than a product and a price. It is the full package a buyer weighs: what it is, who it is for, why believe it, what it costs, what reduces the risk, and what to do next. Writing these parts separately exposes the weak or missing ones before money is spent on ads, and gives creative and messaging agents the same raw material.

## The worksheet

```text
OFFER WORKSHEET - [product or group, exact store name]
Date:            Sources checked:

1. AUDIENCE
   Who this is for (from owner or customer evidence):
   Evidence for that:                         [source]
   Label: confirmed / hypothesis

2. PROBLEM OR DESIRE
   What they want to solve or achieve, in their words:
   Where those words came from:               [source]

3. PRODUCT
   What it is, in one plain sentence:
   Key published features relevant to the problem:   [source each]

4. REASONS TO BELIEVE
   Verifiable proof available (published specs, real reviews,
   certifications shown on the store, policies):     [source each]

5. TERMS
   Price and currency:                         [checked: date]
   Delivery, returns, warranty, payment options:     [source each]
   Any current promotion and its real end date:

6. RISK REDUCERS
   What lowers the buyer's risk, as published:

7. CALL TO ACTION
   The single next step and where it leads:

8. MUST NOT SAY
   Claims excluded by policy or by the owner:
```

## Rules for filling it

- Mark every audience or motivation line as confirmed or hypothesis. Why: a guess written as fact gets repeated in every ad.
- Use customer language from real messages or reviews where available, and record where it came from.
- Features go in as published. Benefits must follow honestly from a feature without adding a number the store does not state.
- Range, speed, safety and legality statements follow the [[Verified Claims Policy]] with no exceptions.
- Promotions need a real end date; never invent urgency.
- One call to action per offer. Why: competing next steps reduce action on all of them.

## Using the worksheet

Pass it to the [[Creative Brief Template]] and choose the test variable in [[Ad Test Structure]] from the hypothesis lines. Review it whenever the [[Store Facts Sheet]] changes.

## Facts to confirm from the store

- Exact product names and published features.
- Current price, promotions and their end dates.
- Delivery, return, warranty and payment terms.
- Any reviews or certifications displayed, and whether they may be quoted.
- From the owner: intended audience and claims to avoid.

## Check before you hand off

- All eight sections are filled or marked as open questions.
- Every factual line has a source.
- Audience and motivation lines are labelled confirmed or hypothesis.
- Section 8 lists excluded claims.
