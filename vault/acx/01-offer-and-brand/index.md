# 01 Offer and Brand

> This chapter gives the process for writing down what the brand offers, to whom and why it matters, and for capturing a consistent voice, using only confirmed facts.

## Why this chapter comes first

Ads, pages, videos and replies all restate the same thing: a specific product for a specific person, with specific reasons to believe and a specific next step. If that statement is vague or differs between channels, each agent improvises and customers receive mixed messages. Writing it once, from verified facts, gives every later task a shared starting point and makes test results easier to interpret, because the thing being tested is defined.

## Notes in this chapter

- [[Offer Definition Worksheet]] is a fill-in template covering audience, problem, product, proof, terms and call to action, with each factual line tied to a source.
- [[Brand Voice Guide Process]] describes how to derive voice rules from the brand's existing materials and owner input rather than inventing a personality.

## How to use it

1. Complete or read the [[Store Facts Sheet]].
2. Fill the offer worksheet for each product or product group being promoted.
3. Gather voice evidence and draft the voice guide for human approval.
4. Reference both documents in every brief and reply template.

## Related chapters

## What this chapter does not contain

It contains no positioning for the actual brand. Audience descriptions, benefits and proof points must come from the brand owner, customer conversations and the store. An agent may propose options clearly labelled as hypotheses for a human to choose between, but must not present a guessed audience or benefit as established.

## Outputs expected

- One completed worksheet per promoted product, with sources.
- One voice guide, marked draft until approved.
- A list of open questions for the brand owner.
