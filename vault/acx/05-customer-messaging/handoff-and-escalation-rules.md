# Handoff And Escalation Rules

> Pass a conversation to a responsible human whenever safety, legal questions, money disputes, missing facts or an upset customer are involved, and do it with a full summary so the customer never repeats themselves.

## Why clear rules

An agent that keeps answering beyond what it can verify creates risk; one that hands off everything creates delay. Written triggers remove the judgment call in the cases that matter most, and a standard handoff note keeps the customer's experience smooth.

## Escalate immediately when

- **Safety.** Any report of an accident, injury, fire, overheating, smoke, battery swelling or damage, brake or structural failure. If there is immediate danger, advise the customer to contact local emergency services first. Do not diagnose or give repair instructions.
- **Legal or regulatory questions** beyond approved wording, and any legal threat.
- **Requests to modify** the bike, including derestricting speed or altering the battery or electrics. Do not advise; hand off.
- **Money issues.** Refund or chargeback disputes, payment failures, suspected fraud, requests for exceptions or discounts not published.
- **Warranty or damage claims**, and deliveries that arrived damaged or incomplete.
- **Missing facts.** The answer is not in the [[Store Facts Sheet]] and the customer needs it to decide.
- **Distress or anger**, repeated complaints, or a request to speak to a person.
- **Anything you are unsure about.** Why: a short delay costs less than a wrong answer.

## How to hand off

1. Tell the customer plainly that a team member will take over, and when to expect a reply according to the brand's actual hours. Do not promise an outcome.
2. Write the handoff note.
3. Route it to the person or channel the brand has named.
4. Tag the conversation as handed to human.

```text
HANDOFF NOTE
Customer and contact channel:
Reason for handoff (trigger):
Urgency: immediate / same day / routine
Summary of the conversation so far:
What the customer asked for:
What was already said to them (facts and sources):
Order reference, if given:
Open question for the team:
```

## Rules

- Never minimise a safety report or suggest the customer keeps using a product they describe as faulty.
- Never admit or deny liability, and never promise refunds, replacements or compensation.
- After resolution, add any new verified fact to the facts sheet and note the case for [[Objection Handling Approach]] learning.

These rules sit alongside the [[Verified Claims Policy]] and are applied throughout the [[WhatsApp Lead Reply Flow]].

## Facts to confirm from the store

- Who receives escalations for safety, payments, warranty and general questions, and through which channel.
- Team hours and expected response times.
- The brand's published process for warranty claims and returns.
- Any approved wording for safety incidents.

## Check before you hand off

- The trigger is named in the note.
- The summary lets a human act without rereading the whole chat.
- The customer was told what happens next, without a promised outcome.
- Nothing was said that admits liability, diagnoses a fault or advises on modification.
- The conversation is tagged and routed to the named owner.
