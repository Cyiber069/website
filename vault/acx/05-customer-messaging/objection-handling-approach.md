# Objection Handling Approach

> Treat an objection as a request for information or reassurance: understand the real concern, answer it with verified facts, and accept that not buying is a legitimate outcome.

## Why this approach

A hesitation usually means something is unclear, a risk feels unaddressed, or the product may not suit the person. Pushing past it wins few sales and produces returns, complaints and distrust. Understanding it either resolves the concern honestly or reveals that the product is not the right fit, which is better found before purchase than after.

## The method

1. **Listen and acknowledge.** Restate the concern in your own words. Do not argue or rush to a counterpoint.
2. **Clarify.** Ask one question to find what sits behind it. "Too expensive" may mean budget, value, or comparison with another option.
3. **Answer with verified facts.** Use the [[Store Facts Sheet]]. If the fact is not there, say you will confirm it.
4. **Check.** Ask whether that answers the concern.
5. **Offer the next step or respect the no.** If it is not a fit, say so kindly.

## Common concern types and how to approach them

| Concern | Approach | Never |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Price or value | Clarify what they compare against; state what is included and published payment options | Invent discounts or deadlines |
| Range or performance | Share the published figure and its conditions; link the page | Estimate for their situation or promise a result |
| Safety | Point to published features and the manual; suggest they consider their own experience and conditions | Say it is "safe" or guarantee anything |
| Legal to ride | Explain that rules differ by location and they should check local regulations; use approved wording if provided | State that it is legal, or that no licence or registration is needed |
| Delivery and assembly | Quote the policy and what support exists | Promise dates not in the policy |
| Warranty, repairs, parts | Quote the policy text and how to claim | Extend or reinterpret terms |

The limits in the "Never" column come from the [[Verified Claims Policy]].

## Learning from objections

Log each objection with its type and whether the answer was available. Why: recurring objections show what the product page fails to answer and what content to make. Share the log with those using the [[Product Page Checklist]] and the [[Content Calendar Process]].

## Facts to confirm from the store

- Published payment options and any real, current promotions.
- Published performance figures and their conditions.
- Sizing guidance and stated limits.
- Delivery, assembly, warranty and repair policy text.
- Approved wording for safety and legal questions.

## Check before you hand off

- The concern was restated and clarified before answering.
- The answer used only verified facts.
- No guarantee, estimate, legal statement or invented incentive was given.
- The customer was asked whether the concern was resolved.
- The objection was logged by type.
