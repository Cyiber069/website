# Short-Video Hook Structure

> Build each short video as a hook that earns the next seconds, a quick payoff of what was promised, visible proof, and one clear next step.

## Why structure matters

Viewers decide almost immediately whether to keep watching, usually with the sound off and a thumb ready to scroll. A video that opens with a logo or a slow introduction loses them before the useful part. A simple structure puts the reason to watch first and keeps the promise made, which is what makes people watch a brand's next video too.

## The four parts

| Part | Job | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hook | Give a reason to keep watching in the opening moments | Visual and text together; no logo intro |
| Payoff | Deliver what the hook promised, quickly | Do not stall or tease repeatedly |
| Proof | Show it, do not just say it | Real product, real use, verified facts |
| Call to action | One next step | Say and show it |

## Hook types that stay honest

- **Question the viewer already has.** Taken from real customer messages.
- **A specific problem**, named in the customer's words.
- **A common misunderstanding corrected**, only where the correction is verified.

Avoid hooks that promise something the video does not deliver, shock for its own sake, or imply claims about range, speed, safety or legality. Why: misleading hooks win a few seconds and lose trust, and may breach the [[Verified Claims Policy]].

## Script template

```text
VIDEO SCRIPT
Topic and source question:
Hook (on-screen text + first shot):
Payoff (what is shown or said next):
Proof (shots; each fact with its source):
Call to action (one):
On-screen captions for all speech: yes
Length target and format:
Facts used -> source and date:
Footage and music rights confirmed:
Riding depiction approved:
```

## Production rules

- **Make sense with sound off.** Captions and on-screen text carry the message.
- **One idea per video.** Why: a second idea is a second video.
- **Demonstrate responsibly.** Follow the brand's guidance on protective gear and riding conduct. Do not film riding that the brand has not approved or that local rules may prohibit.

## Testing hooks

Record several hooks for the same body and compare them one at a time. Hook is one of the variables in [[Ad Test Structure]]. Log which hook type each video used in the tracker from [[Content Calendar Process]] so patterns can be seen over time.

## Facts to confirm from the store

- Exact product name and the published features shown or mentioned.
- Any figure spoken or displayed, with the store's conditions.
- From the owner: approved riding depictions, safety gear guidance, and available footage with rights.
- The destination for the call to action.

## Check before you hand off

- The hook's promise is delivered in the video.
- The video is understandable muted.
- Every stated fact has a source and date in the script.
- No unverified range, speed, safety or legality claim appears, in speech, text or implication.
- Rights and riding depiction approvals are recorded.
- The script is marked as awaiting human review.
