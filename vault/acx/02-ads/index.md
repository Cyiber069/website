# 02 Ads

> This chapter gives the process for planning disciplined ad tests, briefing creative clearly, and reporting weekly results without overstating what the numbers show.

## Why process matters in ads

Advertising spends real money on every decision. Without a plan, tests change several things at once, run too briefly, and get judged on whichever number looks best, so nothing is learned and budget drifts toward noise. A simple, repeated routine, one hypothesis, one variable, a decision rule written in advance and an honest weekly review, turns spend into knowledge about what the audience responds to.

## Notes in this chapter

- [[Ad Test Structure]] explains how to state a hypothesis, isolate one variable, set a budget and duration with a human, and decide what happens after the test.
- [[Creative Brief Template]] is the fill-in brief that tells a creative agent or person exactly what to make, with verified facts and required exclusions.
- [[Weekly Ads Reporting]] is the routine and template for a weekly report that separates facts, interpretation and recommendations.

## How to use it

1. Start from an [[Offer Definition Worksheet]].
2. Write the test plan and get approval for budget and any account changes.
3. Brief and produce creative.
4. Launch only with explicit human authorisation.
5. Report weekly and decide by the rule set in advance.

## Guardrails for agents

- Never launch, pause, or change budgets on a live ad account without explicit instruction from the account owner for that specific action.
- All ad copy and visuals follow the [[Verified Claims Policy]]. Advertising platforms also have their own policies; check the current rules of the platform in use.
- Do not use competitor names, customer images or testimonials without permission and a source.
- Do not quote benchmarks or expected results. This playbook has none, and typical figures vary by market and time.
