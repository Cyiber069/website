# Ad Test Structure

> Run each ad test as a written hypothesis that changes one variable, with the success metric, budget, duration and decision rule agreed before launch.

## Why this structure

If two ads differ in audience, image and headline at once, a difference in results cannot be attributed to any of them. If the stopping point is chosen after looking at the data, random swings get mistaken for winners. Fixing the plan first protects the budget and makes each test add to what is known.

## The test plan

```text
AD TEST PLAN
Offer worksheet used:                       Date:
Hypothesis: We believe [audience] will respond better to
            [version B] than [version A] because [reason].
Variable tested (one only): hook / angle / format / audience / offer / landing page
Held constant: everything else, listed here
Primary metric (one):
Supporting metrics:
Budget and duration: [set by the account owner]
Minimum data before judging: [agreed threshold of spend, time or conversions]
Decision rule: if [condition] then [scale / iterate / stop]
Tracking confirmed working on: [date, how]
Approved by:
```

## Rules

- **One variable per test.** Why: attribution.
- **Test in order of likely impact.** Usually the offer and angle first, then the hook, then format and smaller details. Why: polishing wording on a weak angle wastes spend.
- **One primary metric, chosen for the goal.** Use the metric closest to the business outcome that the account can measure reliably. Why: optimising clicks when the goal is purchases rewards the wrong ads.
- **Confirm tracking before launch.** Check that the conversion event fires correctly on the store. Why: a test with broken measurement produces confident nonsense.
- **Agree a minimum amount of data and time, and wait for it.** Include enough days to cover weekday and weekend behaviour. Why: early results swing widely.
- **Do not edit ads mid-test.** Changes can restart the platform's delivery learning and mix two conditions.

## After the test

- Clear winner by the rule: make it the new control and plan the next variable.
- No clear difference: record as inconclusive; do not pick a favourite.
- Both weak: revisit the [[Offer Definition Worksheet]] rather than testing more variations of the same idea.

Record outcomes in [[Weekly Ads Reporting]]. Creative for each version is specified with the [[Creative Brief Template]].

## Facts to confirm from the store

- Which products are in stock and may be advertised.
- Current price, promotion and landing page for each.
- That conversion tracking is installed and which events exist.
- From the owner: budget, markets to target, and who approves launches.

## Check before you hand off

- The plan names exactly one variable and one primary metric.
- Budget, duration and minimum data were set or approved by a human.
- The decision rule was written before launch.
- Tracking was verified and the method noted.
- No account change was made without explicit authorisation.
