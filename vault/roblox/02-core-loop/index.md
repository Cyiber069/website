# 02 Core Loop

> This chapter defines the repeating action-reward-upgrade cycle at the centre of the game and how to make each turn of it feel good and well paced.

## Why this chapter exists

Every retained player is repeating something. If that repeated thing is satisfying on its hundredth turn, progression and content multiply its value. If it is not, no amount of content rescues the game. This chapter is therefore the one to revisit whenever playtests show players leaving without a clear cause.

## Notes in this chapter

- [[Defining The Core Loop]] gives a one-sentence template for the loop, the tests it must pass, and how to prototype it in grey-box form.
- [[Feedback And Juice]] lists the layers of response, sound, motion, numbers, that make each action feel rewarding, and how to keep them cheap on phones.
- [[Session Pacing And Stopping Points]] covers the rhythm of a session: bursts and rests, where players naturally stop, and how to leave them wanting the next visit.

## How to use it

1. Write the loop sentence before writing code.
2. Build it with placeholder parts and test whether it holds attention with zero progression.
3. Add feedback layers, then re-test.
4. Only then move to [[03 Progression and Economy]] to stretch the loop over hours.

## Signs the loop needs work

- Testers stop playing as soon as nobody is watching them.
- Players ask "what do I do now?" after the first upgrade.
- The best strategy is to stand still and wait.
- Upgrades change numbers but not what the player does or sees.
