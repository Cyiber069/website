# Fair Offer Design

> Sell things that speed up or decorate a game that is already fun for free, show offers when the player is looking for them, and never use pressure or confusion.

## What to sell

- **Permanent multipliers and conveniences** as game passes: more earnings, auto-collect, extra storage. Why: clear, lasting value that the buyer feels every session.
- **Time savers** as developer products: currency packs, short boosts, skips. Why: they shorten a wait the player already understands.
- **Cosmetics**: trails, skins, effects. Why: they let players express themselves without affecting balance, and other players seeing them is organic promotion.

Mechanics are in [[Game Passes Versus Developer Products]].

## What not to sell

- **The ability to play.** No energy gates or paywalled core content. Why: free players are most of the community, and an empty server retains nobody.
- **Exclusive power in competitive modes** that free players cannot earn at all. Why: losing to a wallet feels unfair and drives quits.
- **Solutions to problems you created on purpose.** Do not slow the curve to a crawl to sell the cure. Why: players recognise it, and trust lost here does not return. Curves are tuned in [[Cost Curves And Tuning]].

## When to show offers

- After the player has had fun: not before the first rebirth-scale milestone for anything beyond a visible shop button.
- At moments of relevant intent: the storage upgrade when storage is full, the boost when a long goal has just been revealed. Why: an offer that answers a need the player just felt reads as helpful.
- On request: a shop button that is always available and never flashing over gameplay. See [[Menus And Shop Screens]].

## Honesty rules

- State exactly what the purchase gives, in numbers and duration.
- Show the Robux price on the button before the platform prompt.
- No fake countdowns, no fake "last chance", no fake discounts. Why: deceptive urgency is harmful to a young audience and may breach platform rules.
- Limited-time event items are fine when the limit is real and stated.
- Price packs so that larger packs are better value in a simple, checkable way.
- For chance-based rewards, follow [[Paid Random Items Compliance]].

## Respect for young players

Assume many buyers are children. Avoid language that pressures, shames or implies social exclusion for not buying. Why: beyond being the right thing to do, platform policy and parents both react to manipulative design.

## Measuring

Track what proportion of players buy anything and which items, per [[Launch Metrics To Measure]]. If retention drops after adding an offer, remove or soften it. Why: retention funds everything else.

## Check before you hand off

- A free player can reach every core feature.
- Every offer states its effect and price before the purchase prompt.
- No offer appears before the player's first meaningful milestone, other than a passive shop button.
- No artificial urgency or misleading comparison is used.
- Purchase events are logged for conversion measurement.
